Hi there! I'm **Onur Keleş**, a PhD student in the [Department of Linguistics](https://ling.boun.edu.tr) at [Boğaziçi University](http://www.boun.edu.tr), Istanbul, and an [IMPRS](https://www.mpi.nl/department/imprs-graduate-school/13) affiliate at the [Max Planck Institute for Psycholinguistics](https://www.mpi.nl), Nijmegen. I hold an M.A. (GPA: 4.0) and B.A. Summa Cum Laude (GPA: 4.0) from Boğaziçi University.

My research sits at the intersection of cognitive science and NLP, investigating how visual-spatial modality shapes language in deaf signers, hearing gesture users, and vision–language models. I work with [Dr. Kadir Gökgöz](https://linguistics.bogazici.edu.tr/kadir-gokgoz) and [Dr. Nazik Dinçtopal Deniz](https://web.bogazici.edu.tr/nazik.dinctopal/) at Boğaziçi, and collaborate with [Dr. Aslı Özyürek](https://www.mpi.nl/people/ozyurek-asli) and [Dr. Esam Ghaleb](https://esamghaleb.github.io/) at MPI.

<h2>Work in Progress</h2>

<div class="paper-card">
  <div class="paper-card-thumb thumb-contain">
    <a href="/images/papers/gss_fig.jpg"><img src="/images/papers/gss_fig.jpg" alt="Turkish-to-TİD generation pipeline"></a>
  </div>
  <div class="paper-card-body">
    <p class="paper-title">Sign language generation for Turkish Sign Language with LLMs</p>
    <p class="paper-authors">with Inan, M. &amp; Gökgöz, K.</p>
    <div class="paper-venue-row"><span class="badge badge-progress">In progress</span></div>
    <p class="paper-abstract">A modular pipeline that turns Turkish text into Turkish Sign Language (TİD): LLMs translate Turkish into TİD glosses, linguistic modules handle spatial agreement and unknown or ambiguous glosses, and dictionary clips are stitched into poses that drive a 3D avatar. Larger fine-tuned open LLMs outperform prompted closed models on BLEU, chrF, and ROUGE, and Deaf and expert signers evaluate the resulting videos.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="paper-card">
  <div class="paper-card-thumb thumb-contain">
    <a href="/images/papers/metu_context_fig.jpg"><img src="/images/papers/metu_context_fig.jpg" alt="Context length and model size in Turkish reading"></a>
  </div>
  <div class="paper-card-body">
    <p class="paper-title">Interaction of Model Size, Context Length and Text Genre on Turkish Language Models' Prediction of Human Reading</p>
    <p class="paper-authors"><strong>Keleş, O.</strong>, Bektaş Yüksel, S., Kayhan, S. A., Budan, F., Deveci, İ. B., &amp; Dinçtopal Deniz, N.</p>
    <div class="paper-venue-row"><span class="badge badge-chapter">1st National Psycholinguistics Symposium, METU 2026</span><span class="badge badge-progress">In progress</span></div>
    <p class="paper-abstract">Using the Turkish MultiplEYE eye-tracking data (57 readers, 10 passages from four genres), we test how context length, model size, and text genre shape how well language-model surprisal explains reading. Full preceding context fit reading times best, and the smaller Kanarya model (750M) fit better than the larger one (2B), most clearly for rare words. Readers re-read institutional texts the most but understood all genres equally well, and readers whose later reading times tracked surprisal more closely answered more comprehension questions correctly.</p>
  </div>
</div>

<div class="paper-card">
  <div class="paper-card-thumb thumb-contain">
    <a href="/images/papers/tid_reduction_fig.jpg"><img src="/images/papers/tid_reduction_fig.jpg" alt="Signing space"></a>
  </div>
  <div class="paper-card-body">
    <p class="paper-title">Phonetic reduction and referential accessibility in Turkish Sign Language</p>
    <p class="paper-authors">with Dinçtopal Deniz, N. &amp; Gökgöz, K.</p>
    <div class="paper-venue-row"><span class="badge badge-progress">In progress</span></div>
    <p class="paper-abstract">Using pose estimation on narratives from 28 deaf signers, we ask whether discourse context shapes how signs are articulated. Signs that introduce a referent are longer, move more, and use more signing space than later mentions, and they shrink gradually as the referent becomes more accessible.</p>
  </div>
